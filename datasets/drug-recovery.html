A forecast is only the beginning

A shortage report can say “Available” while another field still describes allocation restrictions. The pilot checks the fields together before classifying a later report as qualifying availability.

Newly recovered archives also show why disappearance is not enough: one candidate had a discontinuation notice before it vanished from the observed records. That is different from an unexplained missing row, and different again from proof of actual supply cessation.

What later reports show

Of the 24 historical candidates, 20 later have a qualifying availability report under the revised rule, two do not have one in the observed snapshots, and two have a discontinuation report first. The cases were selected for a pilot; these counts are not a recovery rate or a forecast accuracy estimate.

Version 02 corrects two earlier interpretations that overlooked allocation restrictions in another field. The source itself did not change in those corrections; the reading rule did.

The first prospective collection preserves all 1,615 raw records. A date parser flags 110 rows for further identity and wording review. They are candidates, not 110 independent shortage episodes. One baseline cannot establish a cross-date trend.
