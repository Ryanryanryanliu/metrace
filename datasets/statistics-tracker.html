Economic Data Watch

Know when official statistics change—and whether the new numbers can be compared with the old.

Read the seven-page issueDownload the case index (CSV)

When a release returns, check the series

South Africa’s statistics office says its resumed insolvency release is not a continuation of the earlier series. An explicit historical gap remains, while liquidation statistics continued separately. Publication status alone does not tell you whether the data can be joined.

This first issue turns that kind of distinction into source-linked evidence for researchers and data teams.

Seven cases, five countries

Brazil · Case 01

Brazil's census postponement: what the archived homepage establishes

An extracted archive of IBGE's English homepage, captured on 30 March 2020, contains a notice dated 17 March stating that the census planned for 2020 was moved to 2021. This confirms an announced change of plan, not that enumeration actually took place in 2021.

Why it matters. A revised census plan changes expected data availability. Keep the announcement date, revised plan and eventual completion date separate.

Evidence and limits

The archived homepage establishes the announced revised plan. It does not establish the eventual enumeration year or the detailed reason for the postponement.

Japan · Case 02

Japan's household survey: revised forms and a comparability warning

A Statistics Bureau notice dated 9 March 2018 says revised account books were introduced to half the sampled households in January. It warns about discontinuities in annual changes and contributions, provides an adjustment using propensity scores and inverse-probability weighting, and records retrospective recalculation on 8 February 2019.

Why it matters. When comparing household spending across the change, distinguish raw and adjusted figures and their vintages before interpreting a movement as a behavioural change.

Evidence and limits

The official explanation documents the change and adjustments. This issue does not reconstruct every historical download or date the first public availability of each version.

Greece · Case 03

Greece suspended the GDP flash release, while provisional data remained scheduled

On 4 August 2017, ELSTAT suspended its GDP flash release at 45 days after the quarter and cancelled the second-quarter flash scheduled for 14 August. The same note kept provisional second-quarter data scheduled for 1 September. This is a suspension of the flash publication, not of all GDP statistics.

Why it matters. Different releases of one indicator have different availability. A generic GDP stoppage label would misstate which information was missing.

Evidence and limits

The notice distinguishes the withdrawn flash estimate from the still-scheduled provisional release. It does not prove the actual later publication date.

South Africa · Case 04

South Africa resumed insolvency statistics with an explicit series break

Stats SA says its first resumed monthly insolvency release was published on 21 July 2026, covering June and including a series from January 2023. It explicitly identifies a gap from September 2021 to December 2022 and says the release is not a continuation of the earlier series. Liquidation statistics continued separately.

Why it matters. Resumed publication does not automatically restore a complete continuous series. Distinguish insolvencies of individuals and partnerships, liquidation statistics and the stated gap when joining observations.

Evidence and limits

The article supports resumed publication and an explicit series break. It does not establish the timing of every earlier missed release or the subsequent publication cadence.

China · Case 05

Three surveys, two quarters: six reports under one catch-up episode

In an archived PBOC index captured on 9 July 2024, all six third- and fourth-quarter 2023 reports for the depositor, entrepreneur and banker surveys carry a 22 March 2024 posting label. Six preserved official report pages show the same date.

Why it matters. Reference periods and posting labels are different dimensions. Keeping both helps distinguish the period measured, the date displayed by the publisher and historically evidenced visibility, while avoiding three independent-disruption counts for a shared episode.

Evidence and limits

The displayed posting date is not independently proven first public accessibility. The archive establishes presence by July 2024. The exact due date and delay length remain unconfirmed.

China · Case 06

Two receivables-definition changes: similar names, different measures

The NBS industrial-profits release dated 27 March 2019 explains that monthly accounts receivable was replaced that year by the combined notes-and-accounts-receivable measure. The release dated 27 March 2020 explains a switch back to accounts receivable. Both tie the adjustment to alignment with enterprise financial-statement formats.

Why it matters. These measures should not be joined solely because their names are similar. Recording the definition and the release version in which it changes helps users assess comparability across time.

Evidence and limits

The official releases establish two definition substitutions. This is not a check of every historical value or back series. The two changes remain separate events.

China · Case 07

A collective-enterprise breakdown ended; the aggregate releases continued

The NBS industrial-value-added release dated 14 March 2019 and industrial-profits release dated 27 March both state that the collective-enterprise breakdown would no longer be published from 2019. Both releases still contain their respective aggregate results, so the event concerns withdrawal of a registration-type breakdown.

Why it matters. Users comparing a business category over time need to know when its public breakdown ends. Distinguishing that breakdown from continuing aggregates avoids overstating the data gap.

Evidence and limits

The named breakdown disappears from these releases while aggregate figures continue. That does not establish absence from every other publication channel.

Coverage and interpretation

This issue reviews seven selected cases across China, Japan, Greece, Brazil and South Africa. It draws on an inherited global discovery catalogue of 47 records across 11 countries and a China module of 42 rows representing 40 parent events under its existing rules. The collections overlap and are not an additive global event count.

The original source pilot covered 30 countries. That is a source-search scope, not 30 countries with verified events or an active monitoring service. Only the specific claims in this issue were rechecked; older catalogue judgments remain inherited.

Dates displayed by a publisher, statistical reference periods, archive captures and present-day retrievals are kept separate. Reviews were performed by AI agents against saved source evidence. No recurring alert service is operating as part of this release.
