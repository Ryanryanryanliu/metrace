Research note 01 · Policy Target Watch

When the target changes, the progress story changes

Austria and Finland revised national digital targets in opposite directions. Reading the earlier versions changes how we interpret the distance still to travel.

Metrace · 3 November 2026 · Selected evidence from Austria, Estonia and Finland

Two changes worth keeping in view

Austria’s revised roadmap sets a 2030 target of 80% for people with at least basic digital skills. Its earlier national trajectory specified 100%, qualified by “max.” Finland moved the other way: it raised its 2030 target for small and medium-sized enterprises with at least basic digital intensity from 90% to 95%. These are national targets within the Digital Decade programme; they should be distinguished from the EU-wide targets printed alongside them.

Sources: National strategic roadmap for the Austrian Digital Decade (2023) · Commission State of the Digital Decade 2024 — country reports (Council archive) · National strategic roadmap for the Digital Decade — 2024 Update (2025) · Finland’s National Roadmap — Publications of the Finnish Government 2024:7 · Suomen kansallisen etenemissuunnitelman päivitys (joulukuu 2024) · Finland — Digital Decade full country report2026

A dashboard showing only the current target loses part of this history. A smaller remaining gap can reflect a different destination as well as a different observed value. To understand progress, readers need the target version, its deadline, the population or businesses covered, and the measurement used for the latest observation.

Austria: the earlier target needs a second source

The Austrian case requires care. On page 14, the 2023 roadmap gives the national trajectory as a maximum of 100% by 2030 and also describes an aim of reaching 100%. Elsewhere, a general objective heading gives 80%. Treating either number as self-explanatory would leave a real ambiguity unresolved.

Sources: National strategic roadmap for the Austrian Digital Decade (2023)

The Commission’s 2024 assessment resolves the distinction: its table places 100% in Austria’s national target column and 80% in the EU column, and its accompanying assessment identifies the national target as the more ambitious one. The revised roadmap then explicitly sets Austria’s national target at 80%. We record a decrease of 20 percentage points while retaining the original qualifier and the evidence used to interpret it.

Sources: Commission State of the Digital Decade 2024 — country reports (Council archive) · National strategic roadmap for the Digital Decade — 2024 Update (2025)

The 2026 country report gives 69.8% as the observed result for 2025. That leaves a distance of 10.2 percentage points to the revised target, compared with 30.2 points to the earlier numerical target. Holding the observation fixed makes the role of the revision visible. This arithmetic does not explain why the target changed or establish whether the 2030 commitment will be met.

Sources: Austria Digital Decade Country Report 2026 · National strategic roadmap for the Austrian Digital Decade (2023) · National strategic roadmap for the Digital Decade — 2024 Update (2025)

Finland: a higher target, with a measurement boundary

Finland’s earlier roadmap gives a minimum national SME digital intensity target of 90%. The revised document explicitly raises it to 95%, and the Commission’s 2026 roadmap analysis confirms the increase. The document’s timing also deserves precision: its version label is December 2024, its memorandum date is 15 January 2025, and it says the information was submitted on 29 November 2024. These dates describe different stages.

Sources: Finland’s National Roadmap — Publications of the Finnish Government 2024:7 · Suomen kansallisen etenemissuunnitelman päivitys (joulukuu 2024) · Finland — Digital Decade full country report2026 · Suomen kansallisen etenemissuunnitelman päivitys (joulukuu 2024)

The reported 2025 observation is 94.0%, using version III of the Digital Intensity Index. In that monitoring framework, it is one percentage point below the revised target. We leave the earlier-target comparison disabled: we have not established that the earlier baseline can be read on the same index basis. The numerical target revision is documented; that does not automatically make every historical progress comparison valid.

Sources: Finland — Digital Decade short country report2026, COM(2026)288 annex2 part9/27 · Digital Decade2026 — DESI methodological note · Finland’s National Roadmap — Publications of the Finnish Government 2024:7

Keep the target and the measurement together

The same caution applies when a number stays unchanged. Finland’s earlier roadmap names big data and the revision names data analytics, both with a 75% national target. The official methodology explains that data analytics covers a broader set of technologies. We therefore hold that version comparison for further interpretation instead of classifying it as an unchanged target solely because the percentages match.

Sources: Finland’s National Roadmap — Publications of the Finnish Government 2024:7 · Suomen kansallisen etenemissuunnitelman päivitys (joulukuu 2024) · Digital Decade2026 — DESI methodological note

A useful record preserves the earlier value, revised value, deadline, definition and source page together. It also separates an observed result from an annual trajectory: the latter is an intended path, not evidence that the result occurred. This makes it possible to distinguish a newly specified national target from a change to an existing one, and a measurement change from a policy revision.

What this first edition covers

Policy Target Watch contains 39 selected records across Austria, Estonia and Finland: 22 newly specified national targets, ten unchanged targets, two confirmed numerical adjustments and five records held because evidence or comparability remains unresolved. Countries were selected for the availability of original roadmaps, revisions and progress documents. These counts describe this research sample; they do not estimate how often European governments change targets.

The product links each case to official documents and page locations, with earlier and revised versions kept visible. The 2030 deadline remains ahead, so current gaps describe distance at an intermediate date. They are not final delivery scores. Readers can use the records to ask a more precise question of any progress claim: progress against which target, measured on which basis?

What this evidence can establish

  • This is a selected three-country sample, not a representative estimate of EU target revisions.
  • Targets are national commitments; an EU target is never automatically substituted for a missing national value.
  • Document version labels, memorandum dates, observation years and target deadlines are kept distinct.
  • Historical documents were retrieved in November 2026; their existence in this archive does not establish that Metrace observed them at publication.
  • The original Austrian maximum qualifier and the Commission evidence interpreting it remain part of the claim.
  • Finland’s earlier-target progress gap is withheld because comparability between the earlier baseline and current DII version III observation has not been established.
  • No motives, country rankings, causal effects or final 2030 delivery verdicts are inferred.
